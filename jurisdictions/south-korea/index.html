Environmental permits in South Korea combine media-specific environmental controls with an integrated environmental management system for large-scale pollutant-discharging facilities. The Act on the Integrated Control of Pollutant-Discharging Facilities established an integrated permit system that consolidates permits previously administered under separate air, water, soil, waste, persistent-pollutant, malodor, noise and vibration laws into one facility-level permit for covered businesses.

The integrated permit system applies to business establishments in industry sectors designated by Presidential Decree and operating pollutant-discharging facilities that require permission, approval or reporting under the relevant environmental statutes. The Minister of Environment grants the integrated permit, with functions delegated to regional environmental offices in the applicable administrative structure. The permit applies an integrated assessment of pollution and operating conditions across the business establishment rather than treating each environmental medium in isolation.

Integrated permit conditions are based on the Best Available Techniques Reference Documents and the best environmental management techniques appropriate to each industry and facility. The permit can establish emission limits, monitoring, measurement, facility-operation requirements, waste controls and environmental management obligations. South Korea’s Integrated Environmental Permit System provides an online route supporting the full process from permit application through post-permit management.

For international businesses, permit strategy should be established early in site selection, plant design, industrial classification, construction, installation and operational planning. The integrated permit does not replace all other project decisions. Environmental impact assessment, land-use, building, water, waste, chemicals, energy, transport, industrial safety and sectoral approvals must be identified separately according to the facility, project and location.

Object Definition

Environmental permitting in South Korea is the regulatory approval function through which pollutant-discharging facilities and projects are authorised, conditioned and controlled. It includes integrated environmental permitting for covered large-scale facilities, media-specific air, water, waste and other environmental controls, environmental impact assessment, best available techniques, monitoring, reporting, post-management and material-change requirements.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of South Korea’s integrated permit system is to manage pollutant-discharging facilities as whole business establishments. The system replaces fragmented environmental permits with one integrated decision for covered facilities, applying best environmental management techniques to reduce pollutants and protect human health and the environment while reflecting the technical and economic characteristics of the establishment.

PurposeTo reduce pollutants effectively through integrated control of discharging facilities, whole-establishment environmental assessment, best available techniques, monitoring and enforceable operating conditions.
Primary OutcomeAn integrated environmental permit, revised permit, installation approval, operating report outcome, refusal or another environmental decision defining the facility’s lawful operating position.
Business ValueEarly integrated-control screening, industry and facility mapping, BAT planning and authority engagement support site selection, plant design, installation, operation, investment and long-term compliance management.

Country Characteristics

South Korea’s integrated environmental management system is distinctive because it consolidates multiple prior environmental permits into one business-establishment permit for covered large-scale facilities. The system was introduced in 2017 and integrates controls formerly administered under separate air, water, soil, waste, persistent-pollutant, malodor, noise and vibration laws. The online Integrated Environmental Permit System supports the process from application and technical review through post-permit management.

Operational CultureIntegrated, digital, BAT-based and condition-oriented, with facility-level assessment, technical expert review, sector reference documents, monitoring, post-management and regular permit review central to the system.
Institutional StructureThe Ministry of Environment grants integrated permits and operates the national framework, with regional environmental offices performing delegated functions and local governments or sectoral bodies handling connected approvals.
Permit LogicCovered business establishments in designated industries that install and operate pollutant-discharging facilities requiring permission, approval or reporting must obtain integrated environmental permit before installation and operation.
Language ExpectationKorean is central to applications, technical documentation, permit-system submissions, authority correspondence, public procedure and decisions. English source materials generally require controlled Korean translation.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

The South Korean integrated permit route begins with industry-sector and facility screening. If the business establishment is covered by the integrated control framework, the operator may seek optional prior consultation and then submits an application through the Integrated Environmental Permit System. The Ministry or delegated authority conducts technical review, considers BAT and business-specific conditions, issues the permit and manages post-permit inspection and periodic review.

1. Define the Business EstablishmentIdentify the site, operator, industry sector, capacity, processes, equipment, materials, energy use, discharging facilities, emissions, wastewater, waste, soil and intended changes.
2. Screen Integrated Control StatusAssess whether the business establishment is in an industry designated by Presidential Decree and operates discharging facilities requiring permission, approval or reporting within the integrated-control framework.
3. Map Connected ApprovalsIdentify environmental impact assessment, land-use, building, water, waste, chemicals, energy, industrial-safety, transport and other permits outside the integrated permit scope.
4. Identify the Competent AuthorityMap Ministry of Environment and regional environmental office functions, together with local government and specialist authority roles.
5. Prepare the Evidence BasePrepare technical descriptions, site plans, emissions and discharge data, waste and soil information, best-available-techniques analysis, environmental management material, monitoring plans and supporting documents.
6. Optional Prior ConsultationThe operator may request prior consultation with the Ministry before applying for integrated permit or a permit change.
7. Submit through the Integrated Permit SystemSubmit the application through the online integrated environmental permit system and respond to technical-review or supplementary-information requests.
8. Decision and ConditionsThe Minister or delegated authority grants, conditions, refuses or revises the integrated permit and sets BAT-based operating, monitoring and management requirements.
9. Install, Operate and Manage ChangeInstall facilities, submit report of start of operation where required, operate within conditions, monitor, report, undergo inspection and assess material changes before implementation.
Decision logic: Establish whether the business establishment is subject to integrated control first. Then identify Ministry, regional and local authority roles and map permits outside the integrated scope. The permit record must provide a whole-establishment picture of pollutant-discharging facilities and BAT-based controls.

Cross-Border Relevance

South Korean integrated permits are territorial and establishment-specific, but cross-border relevance is material through international supply chains, industrial exports, regional air quality, coastal and marine environments, foreign investment, energy infrastructure and potential transboundary effects. A South Korean integrated permit must be assessed independently even where an operator holds comparable authorisations elsewhere.

Foreign CompaniesForeign-owned operators are subject to the same South Korean integrated permit, environmental assessment and environmental compliance framework as domestic operators for establishments located in South Korea.
International Industrial ContextSouth Korean manufacturing and industrial facilities frequently participate in international supply chains, making environmental permit conditions, BAT and reporting relevant to multinational operations and customer compliance requirements.
Marine and Regional ContextCoastal and marine environments, shipping, regional air pollution, energy corridors and cross-border environmental effects can be material to project assessment and permit conditions.
Language ConsiderationsKorean is central to permit applications, technical documentation, online system submissions, authority correspondence and decisions. English source material generally requires controlled Korean translation.
Practical RiskAssuming that a foreign permit, group standard, BAT analysis or environmental approval from another jurisdiction automatically authorises the business establishment in South Korea.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central operational risk is incorrect industry or facility classification, resulting in failure to identify the integrated permit route or permits outside its scope. The permit application must align the actual business establishment, process, capacity, discharging facilities, emissions, wastewater, waste and control technology. Incomplete information or unapproved changes can create delay, restrictive conditions or post-permit enforcement exposure.

Scope RiskThe business establishment may be incorrectly assessed as outside the designated industry or pollutant-discharging facility scope, affecting integrated permit need and technical requirements.
Integration RiskIntegrated permit consolidates covered media-specific permits, but environmental impact assessment, building, land-use, water, waste, chemicals, energy and sectoral approvals can remain separately applicable.
BAT RiskFailure to apply appropriate best environmental management techniques or demonstrate effective pollution control can affect permit conditions, monitoring and compliance position.
Evidence RiskIncomplete technical, emissions, discharge, soil, waste, BAT, monitoring or site material can generate supplementary requests, delay or restrictive conditions.
Post-Management RiskIntegrated permits are subject to monitoring, measurement, operating reports, field inspection and periodic post-management; inadequate compliance systems can create enforcement exposure.
Cost DriversTechnical studies, BAT analysis, emissions and discharge modelling, waste and soil assessment, environmental management systems, monitoring equipment, pollution-control technology, online permit process management, legal and technical support, redesign, permit revision and compliance work.

FAQ

What is an integrated environmental permit in South Korea?It is a facility-level permit under the Act on the Integrated Control of Pollutant-Discharging Facilities that consolidates covered environmental permissions into one integrated decision for qualifying business establishments.
Which facilities require integrated permit?Business establishments in industry sectors designated by Presidential Decree that intend to install and operate pollutant-discharging facilities requiring permission, approval or reporting under the relevant environmental statutes require integrated permit.
Which authority grants the integrated permit?The Minister of Environment grants the integrated permit. Functions may be delegated to regional environmental offices under the applicable administrative framework.
What does the integrated permit consolidate?The system integrates permits previously administered under separate environmental statutes governing air, water, soil, waste, persistent pollutants, malodor, noise and vibration for covered facilities.
Can an integrated permit be revised?Yes. The Act provides for permit and revised permit procedures. Changes to discharging facilities, capacity, process, emissions, waste, site or operating conditions can require permit revision or further authority engagement.

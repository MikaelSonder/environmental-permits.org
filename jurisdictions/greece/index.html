Environmental permits in Greece are governed principally by Law No. 4014/2011 on the environmental licensing of projects and activities. The system classifies public- and private-sector projects according to their potential environmental impacts. Category A projects, which may have significant or very significant impacts, are subject to environmental impact assessment and a Decision on Approval of Environmental Terms (AEPO). Category B projects, which have less significant impacts, are generally subject to Standard Environmental Commitments (PPC) rather than an individual AEPO.

Category A is divided into subcategory A1 for projects or activities that may cause very significant environmental impacts and subcategory A2 for projects or activities that may cause significant impacts. The Ministry of Environment and Energy is generally competent for A1 environmental licensing, while Decentralized Administrations are generally competent for A2 projects. The applicable authority and procedure depend on the project category, location, sector and associated environmental requirements.

The AEPO is an autonomous environmental administrative act. It sets conditions, terms, restrictions and differentiations for implementation and operation, including matters such as location, size, technology, technical characteristics, monitoring, mitigation and compensatory measures. It is separate from other licensing systems but operates as a formal prerequisite for subsequent decisions relating to project implementation or operation.

For international businesses, environmental licensing should be assessed early in site selection, development planning, construction, sectoral licensing and operational change management. The AEPO or PPC is not a substitute for all other approvals. Building, planning, water, waste, energy, mining, forestry, coastal, nature, public-health and sector-specific permits must be identified separately for the project.

Object Definition

Environmental permitting in Greece is the regulatory approval function through which public and private projects and activities are classified, assessed and conditioned before implementation or operation. It includes determining the project category, assessing whether an environmental impact assessment is required, obtaining Approval of Environmental Terms for Category A projects or applying Standard Environmental Commitments for Category B projects, and complying with the environmental conditions that apply over the project lifecycle.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of Greek environmental licensing is to identify, evaluate and manage potential environmental impacts before relevant project decisions are made. The AEPO establishes binding environmental terms for Category A projects, while PPC establishes standard commitments for Category B projects. The decision integrates environmental conditions into the wider project authorisation pathway.

PurposeTo protect the environment and support early integration of environmental considerations into public and private project decisions through classification, assessment, conditions, monitoring and administrative supervision.
Primary OutcomeAn Approval of Environmental Terms, Standard Environmental Commitments outcome, amendment, renewal, refusal or another environmental decision defining the lawful project position.
Business ValueEarly project categorisation and authority mapping support site selection, engineering, environmental studies, project schedule, financing, construction, operation and compliance management.

Country Characteristics

Greece uses a project-category system that determines the environmental route. Category A projects require individual environmental licensing; A1 concerns very significant impacts and A2 significant impacts. Category B projects are subject to standard environmental commitments. The AEPO is an autonomous environmental act that is formally required before other implementation or operating decisions can be issued, but it remains legally distinct from sectoral and operating licences.

Operational CultureClassification-led, evidence-based and condition-oriented, with environmental impact assessment, authority consultation, public participation, environmental terms, regular inspection and project-specific monitoring central to the process.
Institutional StructureThe Ministry of Environment and Energy is generally competent for A1 projects; Decentralized Administrations are generally competent for A2 projects; Regional and municipal bodies manage connected local, planning and sectoral procedures.
Permit LogicCategory A1 and A2 projects require environmental impact assessment and an AEPO. Category B projects generally follow the Standard Environmental Commitments route.
Language ExpectationGreek is central to applications, technical documentation, public procedure, authority correspondence and environmental decisions. English source materials generally require controlled Greek-language versions.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

The Greek route begins with project classification. The developer determines whether the activity falls in Category A1, A2 or B. Category A projects require environmental impact assessment and an AEPO, while Category B projects follow Standard Environmental Commitments. The competent authority reviews the record, consults relevant bodies and establishes environmental terms for the project.

1. Define the ProjectIdentify the site, developer, project, capacity, construction, processes, equipment, materials, energy use, emissions, discharges, waste, water, noise, nature, coastal and intended changes.
2. Classify the ActivityAssess whether the project or activity is classified as A1, A2 or B according to its potential environmental impacts and applicable classification rules.
3. Identify the Environmental RouteDetermine whether the project requires EIA and AEPO or whether Standard Environmental Commitments apply.
4. Identify the Competent AuthorityMap Ministry competence for A1, Decentralized Administration competence for A2 and regional, municipal, water, nature and sectoral authority roles.
5. Map Connected ApprovalsIdentify planning, building, water, waste, nature, forestry, mining, energy, coastal, heritage, public-health and other project-specific permits.
6. Prepare the Evidence BasePrepare environmental impact assessment documentation, technical descriptions, site plans, emissions and waste data, water and nature material, alternatives, mitigation and monitoring proposals.
7. Submit and ParticipateSubmit the application to the competent authority, respond to completeness requests and participate in authority consultation and public procedures where applicable.
8. Decision and Environmental TermsThe authority issues, conditions, refuses, renews or modifies the AEPO or confirms the applicable Standard Environmental Commitments route.
9. Implement, Monitor and Manage ChangeImplement the project and operate within environmental terms, conduct monitoring, meet reporting duties and assess material changes before implementation.
Decision logic: Establish A1, A2 or B classification first. Then identify whether EIA and AEPO or Standard Environmental Commitments apply, together with the competent authority and connected approvals. The environmental record should consistently describe the actual project, site and environmental controls.

Cross-Border Relevance

Greek environmental permits are territorial and project-specific, but Greece has substantial cross-border relevance through EU environmental law, islands, coastal and marine environments, regional energy and transport corridors, international investment, shared waters and potential transboundary effects. An AEPO or PPC outcome must be assessed independently even where an operator holds comparable authorisations elsewhere.

Foreign CompaniesForeign-owned developers and operators are subject to the same Greek environmental licensing, AEPO, PPC and compliance framework as domestic project proponents.
EU ContextGreek environmental licensing operates alongside EU-derived environmental assessment, industrial-emissions, water, waste, habitats, air-quality and public-participation requirements.
Marine and Regional ContextIslands, coastal zones, marine environments, regional energy and transport infrastructure, cross-border waters and protected areas can materially affect project assessment and environmental terms.
Language ConsiderationsGreek is central to applications, environmental impact studies, public participation and decisions. English source materials generally require controlled Greek translation.
Practical RiskAssuming that a foreign permit, group standard, environmental assessment or sectoral approval automatically authorises the project or activity under Greek environmental licensing rules.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central operational risk is incorrect classification or failure to treat AEPO as an autonomous prerequisite for Category A projects. A project must be accurately described in terms of location, capacity, technology, emissions, environmental effects and mitigation. Incomplete studies, missed sectoral interfaces or unassessed modifications can delay decisions or create restrictive environmental terms.

Classification RiskA project may be assigned to the wrong A1, A2 or B category, leading to an incorrect EIA, AEPO or PPC route.
Authority RiskMinistry competence for A1 must be distinguished from Decentralized Administration competence for A2, alongside regional, local and specialist authority roles.
Integration RiskAEPO is separate from other project permits but is a formal prerequisite for later implementation or operating decisions; water, waste, planning, building, energy, mining and nature approvals remain relevant.
Evidence RiskIncomplete technical, environmental impact, emissions, water, waste, noise, nature, coastal or monitoring material can result in supplementary requests, delay or restrictive conditions.
Condition RiskAEPO can impose conditions on location, capacity, technology, technical characteristics, emissions, mitigation, monitoring, compensatory measures and future modifications.
Cost DriversEnvironmental impact studies, technical and emissions modelling, water and waste assessment, ecology and coastal work, planning coordination, legal and process management, monitoring systems, mitigation, redesign and appeal work.

FAQ

What is AEPO?AEPO is the Decision on Approval of Environmental Terms. It is the environmental administrative act issued for Category A projects after environmental impact assessment and establishes binding terms for implementation and operation.
What are A1, A2 and B project categories?A1 projects may cause very significant environmental impacts, A2 projects may cause significant impacts and B projects have less significant impacts. Category A projects require EIA and AEPO; Category B projects generally follow Standard Environmental Commitments.
Who issues the AEPO?The Ministry of Environment and Energy is generally competent for A1 projects, while Decentralized Administrations are generally competent for A2 projects, subject to project-specific legal allocation.
Does AEPO replace all other project permits?No. AEPO is an autonomous environmental decision and a formal prerequisite for relevant later project decisions, but planning, building, water, waste, energy, mining, nature and other sectoral approvals can remain separately required.
Can an AEPO require amendment?Yes. Changes in location, capacity, project design, technology, emissions, discharges, waste, water, environmental effects or operating assumptions can require amendment or a new environmental licensing assessment.

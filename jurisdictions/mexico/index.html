Environmental permits in Mexico operate through a federal, state and municipal framework. At federal level, the Secretaría de Medio Ambiente y Recursos Naturales (SEMARNAT) administers environmental impact authorisations, forest-land change authorisations, hazardous waste and certain operating environmental procedures. State and municipal authorities administer many local environmental, land-use, construction, water, waste, air and operating approvals. The project’s sector, location, federal jurisdiction, environmental effects and state or municipal context determine the applicable permit portfolio.

The central development-stage environmental approval is the authorisation in environmental impact matters. Under the General Law of Ecological Balance and Environmental Protection (LGEEPA), environmental impact assessment is the procedure through which SEMARNAT establishes the conditions for works and activities that may cause ecological imbalance or exceed applicable environmental limits. The proponent submits a Manifestación de Impacto Ambiental (MIA) or, where applicable, a preventive report. SEMARNAT may authorise the project, authorise it subject to mitigation or compensation conditions, or deny authorisation.

For operating facilities, environmental obligations can include the Single Environmental Licence (Licencia Ambiental Única) for establishments within federal air-pollution jurisdiction, Annual Operating Certificate reporting, hazardous waste registration or authorisation, water concessions and discharge permits, and sector-specific environmental authorisations. The exact operating route depends on whether the facility falls under federal or state jurisdiction and on its emissions, wastewater, waste, hazardous materials, energy, mining, forest, coastal or other regulated characteristics.

For international businesses, Mexico should be treated as a multi-level permitting environment. Environmental impact authorisation, operating licences and sectoral permits must be sequenced with land, zoning, municipal construction, water, waste, forest, wildlife, energy, mining, port, coastal, industrial and local approvals. A federal SEMARNAT authorisation does not automatically replace state or municipal environmental and development requirements.

Object Definition

Environmental permitting in Mexico is the regulatory approval and compliance function through which projects, facilities and activities with potential environmental effects are assessed, authorised, licensed, permitted and controlled. It includes environmental impact authorisation, preventive reports, operating environmental licences, air emissions, water and wastewater, hazardous waste, forest, wildlife, coastal, energy, mining, monitoring, reporting, inspection and material-change requirements.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of Mexican environmental permitting is to establish environmental conditions before projects and activities proceed and to control pollution and environmental effects during operation. Environmental impact authorisation determines whether a proposed work or activity may be carried out and can impose prevention, mitigation or compensation conditions. Operating permits and licences regulate emissions, waste, wastewater and continuing compliance for facilities in the applicable jurisdiction.

PurposeTo protect the environment and ecosystems through environmental impact assessment, prior authorisation, pollution control, operating licences, conditions, monitoring, inspection and enforcement.
Primary OutcomeAn environmental impact authorisation, preventive report outcome, operating environmental licence, water or discharge permit, waste, forest or wildlife authorisation, amendment, renewal, refusal or other decision.
Business ValueEarly federal, state and municipal mapping supports site selection, project feasibility, design, construction, industrial licensing, operating compliance and long-term risk management.

Country Characteristics

Mexico’s system is distinctive because it combines a federal environmental impact authorisation framework with substantial state and municipal authority over project development, operating controls and local environmental matters. SEMARNAT evaluates environmental impact authorisations within federal jurisdiction and can authorise, conditionally authorise or deny projects. State and municipal authorities can administer separate environmental and development controls, making correct jurisdiction analysis central to project strategy.

Operational CultureMulti-level, assessment-led and condition-based, with MIA, technical review, federal and local authority allocation, public information, mitigation, monitoring, inspections and operating authorisations central to the process.
Institutional StructureSEMARNAT and DGIRA administer federal impact authorisation; PROFEPA supports environmental inspection and enforcement; CONAGUA administers national water matters; state and municipal authorities manage local environmental, land-use, construction and operating requirements.
Permit LogicWorks and activities within the relevant scope require environmental impact authorisation before implementation. Operating facilities can require environmental operating licences, water or discharge permits, hazardous waste authorisations and state or municipal permits depending on jurisdiction.
Language ExpectationSpanish is central to applications, MIA documents, authority correspondence, public consultation, permits and decisions. English source materials generally require controlled Spanish translation.

Applicable Legislation

The General Law of Ecological Balance and Environmental Protection is the central Mexican environmental framework. It establishes environmental impact assessment and the conditions for federal authorisation of works and activities. Its EIA regulation sets procedural requirements for MIA and preventive reports. Water, waste, forest, wildlife, climate, coastal, energy, mining, state and municipal laws provide additional project-specific requirements.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

The Mexican route begins with jurisdiction and project screening. The proponent identifies whether the project falls within federal environmental impact jurisdiction or a state or municipal route. If federal authorisation is required, the proponent prepares MIA or preventive report and submits it to SEMARNAT. The authority reviews the file and may authorise, conditionally authorise or deny the project. The operator then obtains the relevant air, water, waste, forest, wildlife and local operating approvals.

1. Define the Project and FacilityIdentify the site, proponent, operator, state and municipality, project type, capacity, construction, process, emissions, wastewater, waste, water, forest, wildlife, coastal context and intended changes.
2. Identify Federal or State JurisdictionAssess whether the project requires SEMARNAT federal environmental impact authorisation or state or municipal environmental and development approvals.
3. Determine Environmental Impact RouteDetermine whether a Manifestación de Impacto Ambiental, preventive report, environmental risk study or another assessment route applies.
4. Map Connected ApprovalsIdentify operating environmental licence, water concession, discharge permit, hazardous waste, forest land-use change, wildlife, coastal, land, construction, energy, mining and sectoral approvals.
5. Prepare the Evidence BasePrepare MIA or preventive report, technical descriptions, site plans, emissions and wastewater data, waste material, risk study, forest and biodiversity information, mitigation, monitoring and environmental management plans.
6. Submit the ApplicationSubmit federal MIA or preventive report through SEMARNAT electronic or contact-centre processes, or submit state or municipal applications to the competent local authority.
7. Examination and ParticipationThe authority reviews completeness, evaluates environmental effects, may request supplementary information, conducts public consultation where applicable and considers technical and legal requirements.
8. Decision and ConditionsSEMARNAT or the competent authority authorises, conditionally authorises, declines, amends or renews the environmental impact authorisation or related permit and establishes conditions.
9. Construct, Operate and Manage ChangeImplement mitigation and environmental conditions, obtain operating permits, monitor compliance, respond to inspections and assess changes before expansion, modification or project alteration.
Decision logic: Determine federal, state and municipal jurisdiction first. Then identify MIA or preventive-report route and map all water, waste, forest, wildlife, operating, construction and sectoral permits. The project record must align the actual facility, environmental effects, mitigation and operating conditions across all applications.

Cross-Border Relevance

Mexican environmental permits are territorial and project-specific, but cross-border relevance is substantial due to United States border-region industry, transboundary water, Gulf and Pacific marine environments, international investment, energy, mining, ports, manufacturing and supply chains. Mexican permits and environmental authorisations must be assessed independently even where an operator holds comparable approvals elsewhere.

Foreign CompaniesForeign-owned proponents and operators are subject to the same Mexican federal, state, municipal and sectoral environmental approval requirements as domestic entities for projects located in Mexico.
Federal and State AllocationFederal, state and municipal environmental requirements can apply in parallel. The project location, sector, environmental effects and legal jurisdiction determine the approval portfolio.
Border, Marine and Water ContextUnited States border regions, transboundary rivers, Gulf and Pacific marine areas, ports, coastal zones, water scarcity and energy infrastructure can materially affect assessment and permit conditions.
Language ConsiderationsSpanish is central to MIA, permit applications, public consultation and authority decisions. English source materials generally require controlled Spanish translation.
Practical RiskAssuming that a foreign permit, corporate standard, state approval or authorisation from another Mexican jurisdiction automatically authorises the project or facility at the relevant site.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central operational risk is incorrect jurisdiction and permit-portfolio mapping. A project can require federal MIA and authorisation, state and municipal development approvals, water permits, hazardous waste registration or authorisation, forest or wildlife permits and sectoral licences. The technical and environmental record must consistently reflect the actual project and operating configuration.

Jurisdiction RiskFederal, state and municipal authority allocation can differ by project, sector, location, environmental effect and regulated resource.
Impact Assessment RiskProjects requiring MIA or preventive report must secure the relevant environmental impact decision before implementation; high-risk activities can require environmental risk study.
Permit-Portfolio RiskEnvironmental authorisation, operating licence, water, discharge, waste, forest, wildlife, coastal, planning, construction, energy and mining approvals can each be required and are not automatically consolidated.
Evidence RiskIncomplete technical, MIA, risk, emissions, wastewater, waste, forest, wildlife, coastal, mitigation or monitoring material can lead to supplementary requests, delay, restrictive conditions or denial.
Condition RiskEnvironmental authorisations can impose project modifications, prevention, mitigation, compensation, monitoring, reporting, operational limits and future-change requirements.
Cost DriversMIA and technical studies, environmental risk assessment, water and waste analysis, forest and biodiversity work, public consultation, monitoring, mitigation, operating licence administration, legal and process management, redesign, renewal and appeal work.

FAQ

What is a Manifestación de Impacto Ambiental?A Manifestación de Impacto Ambiental is the environmental impact document submitted to SEMARNAT to obtain federal authorisation for works or activities that may cause ecological imbalance or exceed applicable environmental limits and conditions.
What can SEMARNAT decide after reviewing MIA?SEMARNAT may authorise the project as requested, authorise it subject to project modification or prevention, mitigation or compensation measures, or deny authorisation if legal, environmental or information requirements are not met.
Does environmental impact authorisation replace operating permits?No. Environmental impact authorisation is a project-development decision. Operating facilities can separately require environmental operating licences, water or discharge permits, hazardous waste authorisations and state or municipal approvals.
Who issues water-related permits in federal jurisdiction?CONAGUA administers national water concessions, extraction, discharge, wastewater and water-resource matters within the applicable federal water framework.
Can environmental authorisation be amended?Yes. Changes to project scope, location, capacity, process, emissions, water, wastewater, waste, forest, wildlife, coastal effects or operating assumptions can require amendment, new authorisation or updated operating permits.

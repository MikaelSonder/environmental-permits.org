Environmental permits in China are structured around environmental impact assessment for construction projects and the national pollutant discharge permit system for operating polluters. The Environmental Impact Assessment framework applies before project construction and requires environmental effects to be assessed in planning and project approval. The pollutant discharge permit system regulates emissions and pollutant discharges after the relevant facility enters operation.

The Regulation on the Administration of Permitting of Pollutant Discharges established a national permit framework that came into force in 2021. Pollutant dischargers subject to the system must apply for pollutant discharge permits, and unpermitted discharge is prohibited. The system classifies dischargers according to pollutant generation, discharge volume and environmental impact, including key administration and simplified administration categories. For activities not requiring a permit, registration management may apply.

Pollutant discharge permits establish the permitted discharge points, pollutant types, emission and discharge limits, monitoring, recordkeeping, reporting and environmental management requirements for the polluter. Applications and permit decisions are administered through the national pollutant discharge permit management platform. Ecology and environment authorities may inspect production and pollution-control facilities, review self-monitoring and implementation reports, and impose penalties including restrictions, suspension of production or shutdown for violations.

For international businesses, environmental permitting in China should be planned as a staged process: environmental impact assessment before construction; construction and environmental-protection facility implementation; completion environmental-protection acceptance; then pollutant discharge permit or registration before or during operation as required. Provincial, municipal and local ecology and environment authorities, industrial parks, development zones and sectoral regulators can have material roles in implementation and site-specific requirements.

Object Definition

Environmental permitting in China is the regulatory approval and compliance function through which construction projects and pollutant-discharging entities are assessed, authorised, registered and controlled. It includes environmental impact assessment before construction, pollutant discharge permit or registration management during operation, pollutant-control facility requirements, self-monitoring, recordkeeping, implementation reporting, inspection and material-change management.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of Chinese environmental permitting is to integrate environmental protection into construction-project approval and regulate pollutant discharges during operation. EIA determines environmental feasibility and required mitigation before construction. The pollutant discharge permit system establishes operating limits, monitoring and management conditions to control pollution after the facility begins operation.

PurposeTo prevent and control pollution, protect ecological and environmental quality, regulate pollutant-discharge conduct and establish enforceable monitoring, reporting and management duties for polluting entities.
Primary OutcomeAn EIA approval or filing result, pollutant discharge permit, permit registration, completion acceptance result, amendment, renewal, refusal or other decision defining the project or facility’s lawful position.
Business ValueEarly EIA and discharge-permit mapping supports site selection, project feasibility, industrial-park engagement, design, construction, commissioning, monitoring and long-term compliance management.

Country Characteristics

China’s system is distinctive because it combines a construction-stage EIA process with an operation-stage pollutant discharge permit framework. The discharge permit is the central operating compliance instrument for covered pollutant dischargers and is managed through a national online platform. The system uses classified management: key administration applies to entities with relatively substantial pollutant generation, discharge or environmental impact, while other entities may be subject to simplified administration or registration management.

Operational CultureStaged, data-driven and compliance-intensive, with EIA before construction, pollution-control facility implementation, operational permit or registration, self-monitoring, ledgers, public disclosure, implementation reports and post-permit inspection.
Institutional StructureThe Ministry of Ecology and Environment provides national coordination, standards and permit-system administration; provincial, municipal and county ecology and environment authorities implement EIA and discharge-permit functions at the local level.
Permit LogicPollutant dischargers subject to permitting must apply for discharge permit. Key or simplified administration applies according to pollutant generation, discharge volume and environmental impact; registration management may apply to other activities.
Language ExpectationChinese is central to EIA documents, permit applications, online platform submissions, authority correspondence, monitoring reports and decisions. English source materials generally require controlled Chinese translation.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

The Chinese route is staged from project development to operation. The project proponent identifies the EIA route before construction, obtains or completes the necessary project environmental decision, constructs pollution-control facilities with the project, completes required environmental-protection acceptance and then applies for pollutant discharge permit or registration before operation as applicable. Local ecology and environment authorities supervise both review and post-permit compliance.

1. Define the Project and PolluterIdentify the site, investor, operator, project, industrial category, capacity, process, materials, energy use, emissions, wastewater, waste, pollutants, environmental receptors and intended changes.
2. Screen EIA StatusDetermine the environmental impact assessment route required for the construction project before construction and identify the competent ecology and environment authority.
3. Complete Project Environmental ApprovalPrepare and submit EIA documentation or registration as required, complete review and obtain the applicable project environmental decision before construction or project implementation.
4. Design Pollution-Control FacilitiesIntegrate pollution-control facilities and environmental-protection measures with the construction project and align them with EIA commitments and applicable discharge standards.
5. Screen Discharge Permit StatusDetermine whether the operating entity is subject to key administration, simplified administration or registration management for pollutant discharges.
6. Prepare the Evidence BasePrepare facility descriptions, site plans, emissions and discharge data, waste information, pollution-control equipment, monitoring plans, environmental management ledger and permit application material.
7. Apply through National PlatformSubmit pollutant discharge permit application or registration through the national permit management platform and respond to authority requests for supplementary information.
8. Permit Decision and Completion AcceptanceThe authority issues, conditions, refuses, modifies or renews the pollutant discharge permit, while the project completes required environmental-protection acceptance before or upon operating transition.
9. Operate, Monitor and Manage ChangeOperate within permit limits, maintain pollution-control facilities, conduct self-monitoring, keep records, submit implementation reports, disclose required information and assess changes before implementation.
Decision logic: Separate construction-stage EIA from operating-stage discharge control first. Then determine permit or registration category, competent local authority and industrial standards. The operating record must align the constructed facility, pollutant sources, control equipment and monitoring programme with the approved project and permit conditions.

Cross-Border Relevance

Chinese environmental permits are territorial and facility-specific, but cross-border relevance is substantial due to international supply chains, foreign investment, regional air pollution, transboundary rivers, marine and coastal environments, energy and transport corridors and global customer environmental requirements. Chinese EIA and pollutant discharge permit requirements must be assessed independently even where the operator holds comparable approvals elsewhere.

Foreign CompaniesForeign-owned operators are subject to the same Chinese EIA, pollutant discharge permit, registration, monitoring and environmental compliance framework as domestic operators for projects and facilities located in China.
National and Local AllocationNational law establishes the core framework, while provincial, municipal and county ecology and environment authorities implement project EIA, permits, inspections and local compliance requirements.
Industrial Park ContextIndustrial parks, development zones and local authorities can have site-specific environmental access, infrastructure, emissions, wastewater and compliance conditions that affect project feasibility.
Language ConsiderationsChinese is central to EIA documents, permit applications, national platform records, monitoring reports and authority decisions. English source materials generally require controlled Chinese translation.
Practical RiskAssuming that a foreign permit, corporate standard, environmental assessment or approval from another jurisdiction automatically authorises construction or pollutant discharge in China.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central operational risk is failure to manage the staged relationship between EIA, construction, environmental-protection facility implementation, completion acceptance and discharge permitting. A facility may not lawfully discharge pollutants without the required permit. The project and permit records must remain aligned with the actual plant, capacity, processes, pollutant sources, control facilities and monitoring programme.

EIA Sequencing RiskConstruction projects beginning or operating without completing required environmental impact assessment procedures can be subject to suspension and corrective action.
Permit Classification RiskThe polluter may be incorrectly classified as not requiring permit, or the wrong key, simplified or registration management route may be selected.
Unpermitted Discharge RiskPollutant dischargers subject to permitting must apply for pollutant discharge permit; unpermitted pollutant discharge is prohibited.
Evidence RiskIncomplete project, emissions, wastewater, waste, pollution-control, monitoring or site information can result in supplementary requests, delay, denial or restrictive permit conditions.
Monitoring and Reporting RiskPermit holders must self-monitor, keep data, establish environmental management records, submit implementation reports and disclose required pollutant discharge information.
Cost DriversEIA studies, technical design, emissions and discharge analysis, pollution-control facility investment, industrial standards review, monitoring and automatic monitoring systems, completion acceptance, permit platform administration, reporting, legal and process management, redesign and enforcement remediation.

What is the difference between EIA and pollutant discharge permit?EIA is the construction-project environmental assessment process undertaken before project implementation. A pollutant discharge permit regulates operating-stage pollutant discharge, monitoring, reporting and environmental management of the polluting entity. Who must apply for a pollutant discharge permit?Pollutant dischargers subject to permitting under the law must apply for a permit. The system uses key administration, simplified administration and registration management according to pollutant generation, discharge volume and environmental impact. Can pollutants be discharged without a permit?No. Unpermitted discharge of pollutants is prohibited for entities subject to the pollutant discharge permit system. How long is a pollutant discharge permit valid?The cited Regulation on the Administration of Permitting of Pollutant Discharges provides that a pollutant discharge permit is valid for five years, with renewal required before expiry under the applicable procedure. Can a pollutant discharge permit be amended?Yes. Changes in facility, capacity, process, pollutants, emissions, discharge outlets, waste, control equipment or operating conditions can require permit amendment, new application or updated registration.

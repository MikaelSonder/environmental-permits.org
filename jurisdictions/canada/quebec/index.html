Environmental permitting in Québec is organised around an environmental authorization scheme under the Environment Quality Act (EQA, Loi sur la qualité de l’environnement). Québec uses a risk-based framework in which projects can require prior ministerial authorization, a declaration of compliance, exemption or other authorization depending on the activity and its potential environmental impact. The framework is administered by the ministère de l’Environnement, de la Lutte contre les changements climatiques, de la Faune et des Parcs (MELCCFP), together with municipal, regional, Indigenous, federal and sectoral authorities.

Section 22 of the EQA is the central trigger for prior ministerial authorization. Subject to statutory and regulatory exceptions, ministerial authorization is required before a project involving specified activities, including industrial establishments, water withdrawal, waterworks and sewer systems, stormwater management, work in wetlands and water environments, hazardous materials, air-pollution-control equipment, residual-material disposal or recovery facilities and other activities likely to release contaminants or affect environmental quality. Québec’s Regulation respecting the regulatory scheme applying to activities on the basis of their environmental impact (REAFIE) specifies activity classifications, application requirements, exemptions, declarations and authorisation conditions.

Projects presenting lower environmental risk may be prescribed for declaration of compliance rather than prior ministerial authorization, subject to the applicable regulation and filing requirements. Projects that are automatically exempt or receive an exemption may follow a different route. Larger or designated projects can be subject to Québec’s environmental impact assessment and review procedure under Title II of the EQA. For these projects, the proponent may need to file preliminary information, prepare an impact assessment statement according to ministerial directives and obtain a government authorization or attestation of exemption before the section 22 authorization route proceeds.

Québec authorisation strategy should integrate ministerial authorization with land use, municipal approvals, water, wetlands, forestry, wildlife, mining, energy, transport, infrastructure, contaminated land, building, fire, federal fisheries, navigation, Impact Assessment Act, Indigenous consultation and northern or treaty-land processes. Applications are submitted through Québec’s online environmental-authorizations service. Applicants select the request type and project region, indicate their right to act and must send a copy of a ministerial-authorization application to the affected municipality according to municipal directions.

Object Definition

Environmental permitting in Québec, generally described as environmental authorization, is the regulatory approval and compliance function through which project proponents obtain the authorization, declaration, exemption, government decision or other permission required under the Environment Quality Act and related regulations. It includes ministerial authorization under section 22, declarations of compliance, exemptions, environmental impact assessment and review, government authorization, certificates of authorization, water, wetlands and hydric environments, industrial activities, contaminants, hazardous materials, residual materials, contaminated land, rehabilitation, monitoring, reporting, inspection, amendment and enforcement.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of Québec’s environmental authorization scheme is to prevent and control environmental effects before projects proceed and to impose conditions for construction, operation, emissions, water, waste, remediation and long-term compliance. The scheme assigns projects to proportionate pathways based on environmental risk: authorization for projects requiring prior ministerial decision, declaration of compliance for designated lower-risk activities, exemption for projects covered by applicable rules and environmental assessment and review for designated higher-impact undertakings.

Jurisdiction Characteristics

Québec is distinctive because its modernized authorization scheme consolidates many prior triggers under section 22 of the EQA and assigns regulatory pathways according to environmental impact. REAFIE provides a detailed activity-based framework. The official Québec guidance identifies ministerial authorization, declaration of compliance and exemption as key routes, and separately addresses environmental assessment and review, contaminated-land rehabilitation and fauna or flora impacts. The ministerial-authorization online process requires the applicant to identify the request type, region and right to act, and to send a copy to the affected municipality according to its directions.

Operational CultureRisk-based, authorization-led and project-integrated, with section 22 triggers, REAFIE classification, ministerial review, online submissions, environmental assessment, municipal notice, technical studies, conditions, mitigation, monitoring, reporting and lifecycle compliance central to project delivery.
Institutional StructureMELCCFP and the Minister administer the EQA authorization scheme and environmental assessment functions. Municipalities receive copies of ministerial-authorization applications. Regional, Indigenous, northern or treaty-land, federal and sectoral bodies can have connected or independent project-approval roles.
Permit LogicActivities are sorted by type and potential environmental impact. Prior ministerial authorization is required for section 22 activities unless an exemption or alternative route applies; designated low-risk activities may use declaration of compliance; projects subject to Title II assessment and review require the relevant government authorization or exemption decision.
Language ExpectationFrench is the official language of Québec administration and is central to applications, technical records, online submissions, municipal communication, public consultation, authorizations and decisions. English source materials should be assessed for French translation and formal-document requirements.

Key Authorities

No single authority issues every Québec project approval. MELCCFP is the principal environmental-authorisation authority, and the Minister issues or refuses ministerial authorizations under the EQA. Environmental assessment functions administer designated project review. Municipalities receive applications and issue local development approvals. Indigenous governments and communities, northern or treaty-land administrators, federal agencies, regional authorities and sectoral regulators can have separate or connected roles involving land, water, fisheries, wildlife, energy, mining, navigation, ports, transport and infrastructure.

Applicable Legislation

Québec environmental authorization is implemented through the Environment Quality Act and its regulations, especially REAFIE. The EQA creates the prior ministerial-authorization trigger in section 22 and the environmental impact assessment and review procedure in Title II. REAFIE classifies activities by environmental impact and establishes requirements for authorizations, declarations and exemptions. Projects may also be subject to other provincial, municipal, Indigenous, northern, federal and sectoral law.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

The Québec route begins with identifying the proponent, project, region, site and activities. The proponent screens EQA section 22 and REAFIE to determine whether prior ministerial authorization, declaration of compliance, exemption or environmental assessment and review applies. The project then maps municipal, regional, Indigenous, northern, federal and sectoral approvals. For ministerial authorization, the proponent uses the online environmental-authorizations service, selects the application type and region, identifies the right to act and sends a copy to the affected municipality.

A Québec ministerial authorization does not automatically authorize activity in another province, United States jurisdiction, territory or country, and equivalent external permissions do not automatically satisfy Québec requirements.

FAQ

What is a ministerial authorization in Québec?It is a prior environmental authorization issued by the Minister under the Environment Quality Act for covered projects and activities, particularly those identified in section 22 or regulated through REAFIE.
What does section 22 of the EQA cover?Section 22 requires prior ministerial authorization for specified activities, including industrial establishments, water withdrawal, waterworks, sewer and stormwater systems, wetlands and hydric-environment work, hazardous materials, air-control equipment, residual-material disposal or recovery and other activities likely to release contaminants or affect environmental quality.
What is REAFIE?REAFIE is the Regulation respecting the regulatory scheme applying to activities on the basis of their environmental impact. It sets a risk-based framework for authorization, declaration of compliance, exemptions and other EQA regulatory routes.
Does every project need ministerial authorization?No. Some projects can be subject to declaration of compliance, an exemption or another route depending on their activity and environmental risk. Designated higher-impact projects can require environmental assessment and review with government authorization or an attestation of exemption.
How are ministerial-authorization applications submitted?Applicants use Québec’s online environmental-authorizations service, select the project and request type, specify the project region, indicate their right to act and provide a copy to the affected municipality according to municipal directions.

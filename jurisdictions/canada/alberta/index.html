Environmental permitting in Alberta is administered through a provincial authorization framework centred on the Environmental Protection and Enhancement Act (EPEA) and the Water Act. EPEA is Alberta’s primary statute for managing regulatory requirements relating to air, water, land and biodiversity. It designates proposed activities for which an approval, registration, code-of-practice notice or other authorization is required. The Water Act separately governs water allocations, licences, approvals and activities affecting water bodies, water flow and aquatic environments. Alberta’s approval system is designed as a coordinated or “single window” process that integrates potential air, land and water effects through the responsible regulatory director.

An EPEA approval can be required before beginning an activity that impacts or may impact the environment. EPEA authorization routes include approvals, registrations, notices under a code of practice, research and reclamation approvals, and other activity-specific permissions. EPEA approvals can address industrial facilities, waste management facilities, municipal waterworks, wastewater and stormwater systems, pesticides, industrial discharges and other prescribed activities. The provincial Authorization Viewer provides public access to Water Act and EPEA authorizations, including approvals, licences, registrations, notifications and related records.

For water, Alberta authorizations are required for many activities involving diversion, use, disturbance or alteration of water. Under the Water Act, approvals can be required before constructing works or undertaking activities within a water body that alter water flow or affect the aquatic environment. Water licences allocate and govern use of water. Projects involving municipal waterworks, wastewater, stormwater, industrial discharges, drainage, wetlands, dams, water withdrawal, ground disturbance or watercourse crossings can require Water Act, EPEA and other approvals, with separate regimes applying to energy-resource activities regulated by the Alberta Energy Regulator.

Alberta project strategy should integrate EPEA and Water Act authorizations with environmental impact assessment, Alberta Energy Regulator requirements, mining, forestry, public lands, municipal land use, building, fire, conservation, reclamation, contaminated sites, Indigenous rights and consultation, federal impact assessment, fisheries, navigation, species, transportation, pipeline, oil and gas and sectoral approvals. A facility may require EPEA approval, registration or code-of-practice route together with Water Act approval or licence, environmental assessment and other project-specific permissions.

Object Definition

Environmental permitting in Alberta, commonly described as environmental authorization or regulatory approval, is the regulatory approval and compliance function through which project proponents obtain and maintain Environmental Protection and Enhancement Act and Water Act permissions and related authorizations. It includes EPEA approvals, registrations, code-of-practice notices, research and reclamation approvals, industrial and municipal environmental authorizations, Water Act approvals and licences, environmental assessment, monitoring, reporting, public notice, inspection, amendment, renewal, transfer, enforcement and closure requirements.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of Alberta environmental authorization is to protect, enhance and support wise use of the environment through prior regulatory approval and enforceable operating conditions. EPEA manages air, water, land and biodiversity impacts by designating activities that need approval, registration or other authorization. Water Act approvals protect water bodies and aquatic environments, while licences allocate water use. Environmental assessment is intended to incorporate environmental protection and sustainable development early in project planning and to assess environmental, social, economic and cultural consequences and mitigation.

Jurisdiction Characteristics

Alberta is distinctive because EPEA provides an integrated environmental-management framework intended to coordinate review of potential air, land and water impacts through a single-window approach. EPEA approvals are required for designated activities and can be supplemented by registrations and code-of-practice notices for prescribed activities. Water Act permissions remain separately important for water allocation, diversion, works and activities affecting water bodies. The provincial Authorization Viewer makes available Water Act and EPEA approvals, licences, registrations, notifications, amendments, cancellations and transfers, providing a central public authorization record.

Operational CultureIntegrated, project-based and condition-led, with EPEA and Water Act screening, single-window coordination, technical air-land-water review, environmental assessment, public notice, monitoring, reporting, reclamation, financial security, amendments and enforceable operating conditions central to project delivery.
Institutional StructureAlberta Environment and Protected Areas administers core EPEA and Water Act authorization functions through regulatory approvals processes. The Alberta Energy Regulator administers environmental and resource authorizations for energy-resource activities within its mandate. Local governments, Indigenous governments and communities, federal agencies and sectoral authorities have connected or independent roles.
Permit LogicActivities designated by EPEA regulations require approval, registration or a code-of-practice notice. Activities affecting water bodies, water flow or aquatic environment can require Water Act approval; water use can require a Water Act licence. Environmental assessment can be mandatory, exempted or discretionary depending on project type and applicable regulations.
Language ExpectationEnglish is the primary language for applications, technical documents, public notices, authorizations, monitoring and decisions. Indigenous-language engagement, accessible communication, interpretation and translation can be relevant to project-specific consultation, rights-holder engagement and community processes.

Key Authorities

No single authority issues every Alberta project approval. Alberta Environment and Protected Areas administers central EPEA and Water Act authorization functions. The Alberta Energy Regulator regulates energy-resource activities within its mandate and can administer related EPEA and Water Act authorizations. The Environmental Appeals Board hears certain appeals. Municipalities, Indigenous governments and communities, federal agencies, water authorities, mining, forestry, transport, pipeline and sectoral regulators can have independent or connected authority.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

The Alberta route begins with defining the proponent, project, site, environmental effects and competent authority. The proponent identifies whether the activity is designated under EPEA, whether Water Act approval or licence is needed, whether environmental assessment applies and whether Alberta Environment and Protected Areas, the Alberta Energy Regulator or another authority is responsible. The project then prepares integrated air, land and water evidence, files the application, participates in notice and review where applicable and implements authorization conditions before and during operation.

1. Define the Proponent, Project and SiteIdentify the applicant, owner, operator, municipality, site, project footprint, industry, capacity, construction, process, air, land, water, biodiversity, wastewater, stormwater, waste, water use, water body, energy or resource context, Indigenous interests and intended changes.
2. Identify Environmental Effects and AuthoritiesMap EPEA, Water Act, environmental assessment, Alberta Environment and Protected Areas, AER, municipal, Indigenous, federal, public lands, mining, forestry, transport, pipeline and sectoral requirements.
3. Determine EPEA Authorization RouteAssess whether the designated activity requires EPEA approval, registration, code-of-practice notice, research or reclamation authorization or another environmental permission before construction or operation.
4. Determine Water Act RouteAssess whether the project requires Water Act approval for works or activities affecting a water body, or a Water Act licence for diversion or allocation and use of water.
5. Screen Environmental Assessment and Energy RequirementsDetermine whether environmental assessment is mandatory, exempted or discretionary and whether AER, mining, public lands, forestry, pipeline, energy, federal impact, fisheries, navigation or Indigenous processes apply.
6. Prepare Technical and Environmental EvidencePrepare project descriptions, site and engineering plans, air-land-water studies, wastewater and stormwater information, waste material, water and hydrogeological analysis, biodiversity, reclamation, environmental assessment, Indigenous engagement, mitigation, monitoring, financial security and corporate-authority documents.
7. Submit, Notice and Complete ReviewSubmit the authorization application and fees through the prescribed provincial or AER process. The authority assesses completeness, technical and environmental requirements, public notice, statements of concern, Indigenous and interagency matters and may request further information.
8. Decision and ConditionsThe director or competent authority issues, registers, conditions, amends, renews, transfers, cancels or refuses the EPEA approval, Water Act approval or licence, environmental assessment decision, reclamation authorization or related permission.
9. Construct, Operate and Manage ChangeImplement conditions, controls, monitoring and reporting; maintain records and financial security where required; respond to inspections; and assess amendment, new authorization, environmental assessment update or other approval before material changes or closure.
Decision logic: Identify EPEA-designated activity, Water Act effects, environmental assessment status and the responsible regulator first. Then select approval, registration, code-of-practice or licence route and coordinate it with AER, municipal, Indigenous, federal and sectoral processes. Alberta’s single-window approach seeks to integrate air, land and water review, but connected approvals remain distinct.

Cross-Border Relevance

Alberta environmental authorizations are territorial and project-specific, but cross-border relevance is substantial due to Saskatchewan, British Columbia, Northwest Territories and United States boundaries, transboundary rivers, oil sands, energy networks, pipelines, mining, agriculture, Indigenous territories, international investment and global supply chains. An Alberta EPEA or Water Act authorization does not automatically authorize activity in another province, territory, United States jurisdiction or country, and external permissions do not automatically satisfy Alberta requirements.

Domestic and International OperatorsAlberta, Canadian, United States, foreign-owned and multinational proponents are generally subject to the same EPEA, Water Act, AER, municipal, Indigenous, federal and sectoral authorization requirements for activities located in Alberta.

Environmental permitting in Georgia is administered principally by the Environmental Protection Division (EPD) of the Georgia Department of Natural Resources. EPD is charged with protecting Georgia’s air, land and water resources through state and federal environmental statutes. Its programmes regulate public and private facilities in areas including air quality, water quality, hazardous waste, water supply, solid waste, surface mining, underground storage tanks and related environmental activities. EPD issues and enforces state permits in these areas and administers delegated federal environmental permits except federal Clean Water Act Section 404 wetlands permits.

For air, Georgia’s State Implementation Plan (SIP) Construction and Operating Permit Program is used for initial air-quality permits and modifications to existing air permits. Depending on the facility and source category, an operator may require a construction permit, operating permit, Title V permit, synthetic minor or other source-specific air authorisation. Applications and permitting information are administered through EPD’s Air Protection Branch, with the Georgia EPD Online System (GEOS) designed to streamline electronic permit applications, reports and payment functions for eligible programmes.

For wastewater, EPD’s Wastewater Regulatory Program has delegated authority from the United States Environmental Protection Agency to administer permits for treatment and disposal of domestic and industrial wastewater under the Clean Water Act. A permit is required for a facility intending to discharge, land apply or inject wastewater or fluid within Georgia. NPDES permits apply to point sources discharging or intending to discharge to waters of the state; land application system (LAS), underground injection control and general permit routes may apply depending on the disposal method and facility. New wastewater facilities seeking discharge or land-application permits must first submit the New Facility form before the permit application route in GEOS.

Georgia project strategy should integrate EPD authorisations with local zoning, building, fire, utility, water withdrawal, erosion and sedimentation, dam safety, mining, transport, energy, oil and gas, coastal, federal wetlands, endangered-species, historic-preservation and sectoral approvals. A facility can require multiple air, water, stormwater, waste, hazardous-material, storage-tank, water-supply and local or federal permissions. No single EPD permit generally substitutes for the complete project approval portfolio.

The purpose of Georgia environmental permitting is to protect air, land and water resources through prior authorisation, technical review and enforceable operating conditions for regulated facilities and activities. EPD’s programmes regulate air emissions, treatment and disposal of wastewater, water discharges, land application, injection, stormwater, waste, hazardous waste, drinking water, mining and storage activities. Jurisdiction Characteristics

Georgia is distinctive because EPD centrally administers a wide range of state environmental permits across air, water and land programmes and has broad delegation for federal environmental permitting other than Clean Water Act Section 404 wetland permits. EPD’s Air Protection, Watershed Protection and Land Protection functions manage distinct permit portfolios. GEOS is the electronic portal for eligible permit applications, notices, reports and payments, including Title V, NPDES municipal wastewater, construction and industrial stormwater, hazardous waste, underground storage tanks, brownfields, voluntary remediation, solid waste and other listed programmes.

Operational CultureBranch-based, technical and electronically administered, with air construction and operating approval, wastewater permit routes, general permits, site-specific permits, engineering evidence, monitoring, reporting, compliance and GEOS submissions central to project delivery.
Institutional StructureEPD is the principal state environmental regulator within the Georgia Department of Natural Resources. The Air Protection Branch manages air authorisations; the Watershed Protection Branch administers wastewater, water supply, stormwater, water withdrawal, dam safety and related programmes; the Land Protection Branch manages waste, hazardous waste, mining and storage-related environmental programmes.
Permit LogicFacilities are classified by regulated medium and activity. New or modified air sources may need construction and operating permits; wastewater facilities may require NPDES, LAS or injection authorisation; stormwater and waste programmes may offer general permits, individual permits, registrations or notices according to eligibility and risk.
Language ExpectationEnglish is the primary language for EPD applications, GEOS submissions, technical documents, public notices, permits, monitoring, reporting and decisions. Project-specific public participation or community-engagement requirements can call for accessible outreach, translated information or interpretation.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

The Georgia route begins with facility and media screening. The proponent identifies the site, facility, air sources, wastewater route, stormwater, water supply, waste streams, land and mining elements and connected authorities. The team then determines which EPD branch and permit route applies: air construction or operating permit; NPDES, LAS or injection authorisation; general or individual stormwater permit; waste, hazardous-waste, mining or storage approval; and local, federal and sectoral permits.

1. Define the Facility and ProjectIdentify the applicant, owner, operator, county, municipality, site, industry, equipment, processes, capacity, construction, emissions, water, wastewater, stormwater, land application, injection, waste, hazardous materials, water supply, mining, storage and intended changes.
2. Identify EPD Branches and Other AuthoritiesMap Air Protection, Watershed Protection and Land Protection requirements, GEOS eligibility, district office contacts, local development approvals, federal wetlands, water, coastal, utility, mining, transport and sectoral authorities.
3. Determine Air Permit RouteAssess SIP construction and operating permit requirements, Title V, source modifications, emissions controls, registration, reporting and other Air Protection Branch authorisations before equipment installation or operation.
4. Determine Wastewater and Water RouteAssess NPDES, LAS, underground injection, municipal or industrial wastewater, stormwater, water supply, water withdrawal, drinking water, dam safety and related Watershed Protection requirements.
5. Complete New Facility and General Permit StepsFor new facilities seeking wastewater discharge or land-application permits, submit the Wastewater Regulatory Program New Facility form, then submit permit applications, notices of intent or notices of termination through GEOS as applicable.
6. Determine Land and Waste RouteAssess hazardous waste, solid waste, landfill, recycling, mining, storage tank, brownfield, remediation and related Land Protection Branch permit, registration or compliance requirements.
7. Prepare Technical Evidence and SubmitPrepare applications, site and engineering plans, equipment lists, emissions calculations, wastewater and stormwater data, waste classification, water-supply information, monitoring and corporate-authority documents; submit through GEOS or the required EPD pathway.
8. Review, Decision and ConditionsEPD and other authorities review completeness, technical and legal requirements, public notice where applicable and issue, condition, amend, renew, register or deny the relevant authorisation.
9. Construct, Operate and Manage ChangeImplement controls and permit conditions, monitor and report, maintain records, respond to inspections and assess amendment, renewal, new permit or revised general-permit coverage before material changes.
Decision logic: Identify every regulated media, the appropriate EPD branch and all connected authorities first. Then determine whether the project needs air construction or operating permits, NPDES, LAS, injection, stormwater, waste, mining or other authorisation. Align applications, engineering and actual facility operations before construction, discharge or operation begins.

Cross-Border Relevance

Georgia environmental authorisations are territorial and facility-specific, but cross-border relevance is substantial due to the Atlantic coast, Savannah port, interstate watersheds, Tennessee Valley, southeastern energy and manufacturing, agriculture, logistics, foreign investment and international supply chains. A federal, foreign, out-of-state or other-site authorisation does not automatically satisfy Georgia EPD or local requirements.

Foreign and Interstate BusinessesForeign-owned and out-of-state proponents are generally subject to the same Georgia environmental permitting, monitoring, reporting and compliance framework as Georgia-based operators for activities located in the state.
Coastal, Port and Interstate Water ContextAtlantic coast, Savannah and other ports, coastal resources, interstate watersheds, river basins, transportation corridors, energy networks and industrial supply chains can require additional local, state, federal, water, coastal or sectoral analysis.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central operational risk is incomplete permit-portfolio and disposal-route analysis. A facility may need air construction and operating permits, Title V, NPDES or LAS authority, injection or stormwater coverage, water-supply approvals, waste permits, storage-tank requirements, local development approvals and federal wetlands permits. The facility record must consistently reflect actual equipment, emissions, wastewater, disposal method, waste streams, control systems and intended operations.

Air Route RiskNew and modified air sources can require SIP construction and operating permits, Title V or another air authorisation. Source type, emissions, equipment and operational changes determine the appropriate route.
Wastewater Disposal RiskDischarge, land application and injection are different wastewater disposal routes and can require NPDES, LAS, injection or other permits with distinct technical, monitoring and operating conditions.
General versus Individual Permit RiskStormwater and other qualifying activities may use general permits, notices of intent and electronic filing, while non-qualifying or complex facilities can require individual, site-specific authorisation.
Multi-Branch RiskAir, watershed and land protection programmes are separate. A single project can require permits from multiple EPD branches alongside local, federal and sectoral agencies.
Evidence RiskIncomplete or inconsistent facility, emissions, wastewater, land-application, injection, stormwater, waste, engineering, site, monitoring, public notice or corporate-authority evidence can delay review or result in restrictive conditions.
Cost DriversEngineering, emissions calculations, air controls, wastewater and stormwater design, land-application or injection studies, waste characterisation, mining or storage planning, consultants, permit fees, GEOS administration, monitoring systems, reporting, compliance staff, amendments, renewals and remediation work.

FAQ

Which agency issues major environmental permits in Georgia?Georgia EPD is the principal state environmental authority. It issues and enforces state permits for air quality, water quality, hazardous waste, water supply, solid waste, surface mining, underground storage tanks and other regulated areas.
What air permit system does Georgia use?Georgia’s SIP Construction and Operating Permit Program is used for initial air-quality permits and modifications to existing air permits. Title V and other source-specific air authorisations may also apply depending on the facility.
When is a wastewater permit required?EPD requires a permit for any facility that intends to discharge, land apply or inject wastewater or fluid within Georgia. The relevant route may be NPDES, LAS, underground injection control, general permit or another wastewater authorisation.
When is an NPDES permit required?Any point source that discharges or intends to discharge to waters of the state must obtain an NPDES permit under the applicable EPD programme.
Can project changes require further permit action?Yes. Changes to site, equipment, process, capacity, emissions, wastewater, disposal route, stormwater, waste, water supply, mining, storage or operating conditions can require permit amendment, new permit, revised notice, updated general-permit coverage or other authorisation before implementation.

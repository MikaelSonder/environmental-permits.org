Environmental permits in Romania operate through a staged environmental-regulation system that distinguishes project development from operational authorisation. A project with possible significant environmental effects may require an environmental agreement (acord de mediu) before development, while an existing or new activity subject to environmental control requires an environmental authorisation (autorizaţie de mediu) before it is commissioned and operated.

Installations within the integrated pollution prevention and control framework require an integrated environmental agreement for the development stage and an integrated environmental authorisation (autorizaţie integrată de mediu) for operation. The integrated environmental authorisation is the administrative act that grants the right to operate an installation, in whole or in part, under conditions ensuring compliance with integrated pollution prevention and control requirements. It may cover one or more installations or parts of installations on the same site operated by the same operator.

Environmental agreements, authorisations and integrated environmental authorisations are administered through the National Agency for Environmental Protection system and local or regional environmental-protection agencies. The competent authority depends on the project, site, activity, industrial-emissions status, protected-area context and associated environmental procedures. Operation without an environmental authorisation or, where required, an integrated environmental authorisation is prohibited.

For international businesses, Romanian permitting should be addressed early in site selection, acquisition diligence, design, construction, commissioning and operational change management. The environmental agreement and environmental authorisation are separate decisions with different functions. They must also be coordinated with building, water, waste, nature, industrial, energy, mining, planning and sectoral approvals relevant to the project.

Object Definition

Environmental permitting in Romania is the regulatory approval function through which projects and activities with possible environmental effects are assessed, authorised and controlled. It includes development-stage environmental agreements, operational environmental authorisations and integrated environmental authorisations for installations subject to integrated pollution prevention and control, together with annual endorsement, monitoring, compliance and material-change requirements.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of Romanian environmental permitting is to ensure that projects and activities are developed and operated in conditions that prevent, reduce and control adverse environmental effects. The development-stage environmental agreement defines project conditions, while the operational environmental authorisation or integrated environmental authorisation defines the conditions for commissioning and operation.

PurposeTo protect the environment and human health through prior development assessment, operational authorisation, integrated pollution prevention, conditions, monitoring and administrative supervision.
Primary OutcomeAn environmental agreement, environmental authorisation, integrated environmental authorisation, annual endorsement, amendment, refusal or another decision defining the project’s lawful position.
Business ValueEarly separation of development-stage and operational-stage permit routes supports site selection, design, construction sequencing, commissioning, financing and long-term compliance management.

Country Characteristics

Romania’s system is distinctive because it separates project development approval from operational authorisation. The environmental agreement is associated with development and construction conditions, while the environmental authorisation is required before the activity is put into operation. For IPPC installations, integrated environmental agreements and integrated environmental authorisations apply. Environmental authorisations and integrated authorisations remain valid subject to annual endorsement under the applicable procedure.

Operational CultureStructured, staged and condition-based, with separate development and operating decisions, technical documentation, public information, environmental-agency administration, annual endorsement and compliance monitoring.
Institutional StructureThe National Agency for Environmental Protection and county environmental-protection agencies administer environmental agreements and authorisations, while the Ministry and specialist water, nature and sectoral bodies remain relevant to policy and connected procedures.
Permit LogicProjects with possible significant effects require development-stage environmental assessment and agreement where applicable. Activities subject to authorisation must obtain environmental authorisation before operation; IPPC installations require integrated environmental authorisation.
Language ExpectationRomanian is central to applications, technical documentation, public participation, authority correspondence and decisions. International source materials generally require controlled Romanian-language versions.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

The Romanian route begins by distinguishing the project-development stage from the operating stage. The developer identifies whether environmental assessment and an environmental agreement are required for development. Before an activity is commissioned, the operator determines whether a standard environmental authorisation or an integrated environmental authorisation is required. The competent environmental authority then reviews the evidence, coordinates required opinions and establishes conditions.

1. Define the Project and ActivityIdentify the site, operator, project, capacity, process, construction, equipment, materials, fuels, energy use, emissions, discharges, waste, water, noise, nature context and intended changes.
2. Identify Development-Stage RequirementsAssess whether the project requires environmental assessment and an environmental agreement or integrated environmental agreement before development, construction or building-permit sequencing.
3. Identify Operating-Stage RequirementsDetermine whether the activity requires environmental authorisation before commissioning or, for IPPC installations, integrated environmental authorisation.
4. Identify the Competent AuthorityMap the relevant county environmental-protection agency, ANPM coordination, water, nature, sectoral and local authorities according to project location and activity.
5. Map Connected ApprovalsIdentify building, planning, water, waste, protected-area, energy, mining, public-health, fire, industrial and other permits or opinions required for the project.
6. Prepare the Evidence BasePrepare technical descriptions, site plans, impact and environmental studies, emissions and discharge information, waste material, best-available-techniques analysis, monitoring proposals and supporting documents.
7. Submit and ParticipateSubmit the application to the competent environmental authority, publish or provide public information where required, respond to supplementary requests and participate in consultation procedures.
8. Decision and ConditionsThe authority issues, conditions, refuses, modifies or endorses the environmental agreement, environmental authorisation or integrated environmental authorisation.
9. Operate, Endorse and Manage ChangeOperate within authorisation conditions, obtain annual endorsement where required, monitor and report, respond to inspections and assess changes before implementation.
Decision logic: Separate development from operation first. Then determine whether the activity needs environmental agreement, environmental authorisation or integrated environmental authorisation. The project record must align the actual construction, commissioning and operating model with the environmental conditions sought.

Cross-Border Relevance

Romanian environmental authorisations are territorial and site-specific, but Romania has substantial cross-border relevance through EU environmental law, the Danube and Black Sea environments, shared river basins, international energy and transport links, foreign investment and potential transboundary effects. A Romanian environmental agreement or authorisation must be assessed independently even where an operator holds comparable approvals elsewhere.

Foreign CompaniesForeign-owned operators are subject to the same Romanian environmental agreement, authorisation, integrated authorisation and compliance framework as domestic operators for activities located in Romania.
EU ContextRomanian environmental permitting operates alongside EU-derived industrial-emissions, environmental-assessment, water, waste, habitats, air-quality and public-participation requirements.
Water and Regional ContextDanube, Black Sea, cross-border river-basin, protected-area and regional environmental effects can be material to project assessment, authority coordination and permit conditions.
Language ConsiderationsRomanian is central to applications, technical documentation, public participation and decisions. English source materials generally require controlled Romanian translation.
Practical RiskAssuming that a foreign permit, group standard, environmental assessment or authorisation from another jurisdiction automatically authorises the project or activity in Romania.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central operational risk is failure to distinguish the development-stage and operational-stage approval routes. A project can require an environmental agreement before development and an environmental authorisation before operation, with integrated versions applying to IPPC installations. The permit record must accurately describe the actual project and operating conditions to avoid delay, restrictive conditions or future compliance exposure.

Stage-Selection RiskA developer may treat the environmental agreement as sufficient for operation, or seek an operational authorisation without properly managing development-stage environmental requirements.
IPPC Classification RiskAn installation may be incorrectly assessed as requiring only standard environmental authorisation when integrated environmental authorisation is required under IPPC rules.
Authority RiskCompetence between county environmental agencies, ANPM coordination, water, nature and sectoral bodies must be mapped accurately for the project location and activity.
Evidence RiskIncomplete technical, impact, emissions, discharge, waste, best-available-techniques, monitoring or site material can generate supplements, delay or restrictive conditions.
Endorsement RiskEnvironmental authorisations and integrated environmental authorisations require annual endorsement under the applicable procedure; failure to manage the process can affect the operational permit position.
Cost DriversEnvironmental studies, emissions and discharge analysis, best-available-techniques assessment, water and waste work, environmental impact assessment, annual endorsement administration, legal and process management, monitoring systems, control technology, redesign and appeal work.

FAQ

What is the difference between an environmental agreement and an environmental authorisation?An environmental agreement is associated with the development and construction stage of a project. An environmental authorisation establishes the conditions for operation and is required before an activity subject to authorisation is commissioned.
What is an integrated environmental authorisation?It is the administrative act issued by the competent environmental authority that grants the right to operate an installation, in whole or in part, under conditions ensuring compliance with integrated pollution prevention and control requirements.
Can an activity operate without environmental authorisation?No. Operation without environmental authorisation is prohibited for activities subject to the environmental authorisation procedure. Operation without integrated environmental authorisation is prohibited for activities subject to IPPC legislation.
Which authority issues environmental authorisations?The competent environmental authority, commonly the county environmental protection agency for the installation site, issues the relevant authorisation within the ANPM environmental-administration system. Other authorities may participate for water, nature or sectoral matters.
Do environmental authorisations require annual endorsement?Environmental authorisations and integrated environmental authorisations remain valid subject to annual endorsement under the applicable procedure and require supporting information such as declarations on operating conditions and, where required, annual environmental reporting.

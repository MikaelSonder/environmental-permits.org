Environmental permits in Luxembourg are structured principally through the classified establishments regime, commonly referred to as commodo/incommodo. Natural persons or legal entities intending to operate an establishment or activity listed in the nomenclature of classified establishments must obtain an operating permit. The regime is designed to protect the environment, employees, the public and neighbouring properties from pollution, nuisance, safety risks and other adverse impacts.

Classified establishments are divided into classes 1, 1A, 1B, 2, 3, 3A, 3B and 4. The applicable class determines the competent authority, the type of procedure, public inquiry requirements and whether the establishment is subject to authorisation, simplified authorisation, municipal authorisation or declaration. Class 1 establishments are generally authorised by both the minister responsible for environment and the minister responsible for labour, while other classes may fall under one minister, the Environment Agency, the Inspectorate of Labour and Mines or the mayor of the relevant municipality.

The operating permit sets development and operating conditions necessary to protect the environment and ensure safety for workers, the public and neighbouring areas. The application record typically includes site, cadastral, technical, emissions, waste, water, safety and operational information. The procedure can include public inquiry, municipal notice, publication, specialist opinions, risk assessment and environmental conditions tailored to the establishment class and location.

For international businesses, Luxembourg permit strategy should be addressed before site acquisition, construction, installation, expansion, operational change or start of activity. The commodo/incommodo permit is a central operational authorisation, but it does not replace all planning, building, water, waste, nature, emissions trading, industrial-emissions, public-health or sectoral approvals relevant to the project.

Core Outcome

Object Definition

Environmental permitting in Luxembourg is the regulatory approval function through which classified establishments and listed activities are authorised, declared, conditioned and controlled. It includes classification under the nomenclature, identification of the competent authority, preparation of the operating-permit application, public inquiry and specialist consultation where applicable, compliance with environmental and safety conditions, and management of changes to the establishment.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of the classified-establishment permit is to ensure that establishments which may create pollution, nuisance or safety risks operate only under conditions that protect the environment, workers, the public and the neighbourhood. The decision establishes the technical, operating, monitoring, safety and environmental requirements appropriate to the establishment and its class.

PurposeTo prevent and control environmental pollution, nuisance and safety risks through prior operating authorisation, establishment classification, public procedure, conditions and administrative supervision.
Primary OutcomeA commodo/incommodo operating permit, declaration outcome, amendment, renewal, refusal or related decision defining the establishment’s legally authorised operating position.
Business ValueEarly classification and authority mapping support site selection, design, construction, installation, public-procedure planning, operational compliance and management of future change risk.

Country Characteristics

Luxembourg’s system is distinctive because it combines environmental, worker-safety, public-safety and neighbourhood protection through one classified-establishments framework. The relevant class determines whether authorisation comes from the environment minister, labour minister, municipality or a combination of authorities. The process uses the nomenclature to link establishment type and scale to authority competence, procedure and public-inquiry obligations.

Operational CultureClassification-led, technically documented and condition-based, with integrated consideration of environment, employee safety, public safety, neighbours, public inquiry and operational controls.
Institutional StructureEnvironment Agency, Inspectorate of Labour and Mines, ministers responsible for environment and labour, mayors and communal authorities share competence according to the classified-establishment class.
Permit LogicEstablishments listed in the nomenclature are classified in classes 1, 1A, 1B, 2, 3, 3A, 3B or 4. The class determines whether the route is authorisation, simplified authorisation, municipal decision or declaration.
Language ExpectationFrench, German and Luxembourgish are relevant in public administration. French is frequently central to legal and administrative environmental documentation; applicants should align the file with the competent authority’s requirements.

Key Authorities

Authority competence follows the classified-establishment class. The Environment Agency manages environmental authorisation functions, the Inspectorate of Labour and Mines addresses worker-safety competence, and communes are involved in municipal procedures and public inquiry. A class 1 establishment can require authorisation from both environment and labour ministers, while other classes may involve only one authority or the mayor.

Applicable Legislation

The amended Law of 10 June 1999 relating to classified establishments, known as the commodo law, is the central Luxembourg legal framework. It is supplemented by the Grand-Ducal Regulation containing the nomenclature and classification of establishments. The exact legal route also depends on water, waste, nature, emissions trading, planning, building and sectoral rules relevant to the site and activity.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

The Luxembourg route begins with nomenclature classification. The applicant determines whether the establishment is listed, identifies its class and maps the competent environmental, labour or municipal authority. The application is then prepared with site, technical, safety and environmental information. Public inquiry and specialist review are conducted where required, before the competent authority issues operating conditions.

1. Define the EstablishmentIdentify the site, applicant, operator, activity, capacity, equipment, materials, energy use, emissions, waste, water, noise, safety profile, neighbouring uses and intended changes.
2. Check the NomenclatureAssess whether the establishment or activity is listed in the nomenclature of classified establishments and determine the relevant class.
3. Identify the Authority RouteDetermine whether the establishment requires joint ministerial authorisation, environment authority authorisation, labour authority authorisation, municipal permit or declaration.
4. Map Connected ApprovalsIdentify building, planning, water, waste, nature, emissions-trading, public-health, fire safety, energy and other sectoral permits or opinions.
5. Prepare the Evidence BasePrepare application form, cadastral extract, site plans, technical description, emissions, waste and water information, safety and risk material, monitoring and proposed operating controls.
6. Submit the ApplicationSubmit the application through the competent authority channel, including MyGuichet.lu e-form procedure where available, with required copies and annexes.
7. Public Inquiry and ConsultationWhere required, the commune publishes and displays the project, makes the application available for inspection, receives objections and coordinates authority review.
8. Decision and ConditionsThe competent authority issues, conditions, refuses, modifies or renews the operating permit, declaration outcome or other classified-establishment decision.
9. Operate, Monitor and Manage ChangeOperate within conditions, carry out required checks and monitoring, manage inspections and assess changes to activity, capacity, equipment, emissions, waste or site before implementation.
Decision logic: Determine nomenclature status and class first. Then identify the competent environment, labour or municipal authority and map connected approvals. The application record must consistently describe the establishment, risks, technical controls and local environmental context.

Cross-Border Relevance

Luxembourg classified-establishment permits are territorial and site-specific, but cross-border relevance is material because of Luxembourg’s location, daily cross-border workforce, regional industrial and logistics activity, shared waters and air environments, international ownership and EU environmental framework. A Luxembourg operating permit must be assessed independently even where an operator holds comparable authorisations in a neighbouring state.

Foreign CompaniesForeign-owned operators are subject to the same Luxembourg classified-establishment, environmental and occupational-safety permit framework as domestic operators for activities located in Luxembourg.
EU ContextLuxembourg environmental permitting operates alongside EU-derived industrial-emissions, environmental assessment, water, waste, habitats, air-quality, chemicals and public-participation requirements.
Cross-Border Workforce and ContextWorker protection, public safety, transport, neighbouring communities and cross-border environmental contexts can be material to establishment design, risk assessment and operating conditions.
Language ConsiderationsFrench, German and Luxembourgish can be relevant to the procedure. Applicants should ensure that technical and administrative documentation meets the language expectations of the competent authority.
Practical RiskAssuming that a permit, safety standard, environmental assessment or operating authorisation from a neighbouring state automatically authorises the classified establishment in Luxembourg.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central operational risk is incorrect classification of the establishment or failure to identify the full authority route. The class determines the competent authority, public inquiry, safety obligations and procedure. The application must accurately describe the actual facility, emissions, waste, safety profile and neighbourhood context to avoid delay, restrictive conditions or later compliance exposure.

Classification RiskThe establishment may be assigned the wrong nomenclature class, leading to an incorrect authorisation, declaration or authority route.
Authority RiskEnvironment, labour and municipal competence can overlap or differ by class; a class 1 establishment can require joint ministerial authorisation.
Public-Procedure RiskPublic inquiry, town-hall notice, publication, site display and neighbouring-commune requirements can affect project timing, documentation and stakeholder engagement.
Evidence RiskIncomplete cadastral, site, technical, emissions, water, waste, risk-assessment or monitoring material can generate supplements, delay or restrictive conditions.
Condition RiskOperating permit conditions can affect capacity, equipment, materials, emissions, waste, water, noise, worker safety, monitoring, reporting, inspections and future changes.
Cost DriversTechnical studies, site and cadastral documentation, risk assessment, public inquiry, environmental and safety controls, water and waste work, legal and process management, monitoring, inspections, redesign and appeal work.

FAQ

What is a commodo/incommodo permit?It is the operating permit for a classified establishment under the Luxembourg Law of 10 June 1999. It sets development and operating conditions needed to protect the environment, workers, the public and the neighbourhood.
Which establishments need an operating permit?Natural persons or legal entities intending to operate an establishment or activity listed in the nomenclature of classified establishments must obtain the applicable operating permit or comply with the relevant declaration route.
What do establishment classes mean?Classes 1, 1A, 1B, 2, 3, 3A, 3B and 4 determine the competent authority, procedure, public inquiry requirements and whether authorisation or declaration is required.
Who authorises class 1 establishments?Class 1 establishments are generally authorised jointly by the ministers responsible for environment and labour. Class 1A is generally authorised by the labour minister and class 1B by the environment minister.
Can an operating permit need amendment?Yes. Changes to establishment class, activity, capacity, equipment, materials, emissions, waste, water, safety profile, site or operating conditions can require amendment, new permit or updated declaration.

Environmental permitting in Scotland is administered principally by the Scottish Environment Protection Agency (SEPA), Scotland’s principal environmental regulator. SEPA regulates activities that could cause pollution, environmental damage or resource misuse and uses authorisations to control industrial, water, waste, radioactive-substances and other regulated activities. Scotland is moving to an integrated environmental-authorisation framework under the Environmental Authorisations (Scotland) Regulations 2018 (EASR), which from 1 November 2025 regulates water, waste-management and industrial activities under a unified system.

Under EASR, an activity may be authorised by a permit, registration, notification or compliance with general binding rules, depending on the activity type, scale and environmental risk. Existing authorisations under prior regimes—such as Water Environment (Controlled Activities) Regulations (CAR), Pollution Prevention and Control (PPC) permits and Waste Management Licensing—generally transitioned automatically into EASR authorisations from 1 November 2025. Operators should verify the specific transition status, applicable authorisation category and current SEPA requirements for each activity and site.

Water activities remain a major component of Scottish environmental regulation. Activities involving sewage and organic effluents, fish farms, waste sheep dip, waste pesticides, private sewage treatment, abstractions, impoundments, engineering works and other impacts on the water environment can require general binding rules, registration, simple licence or complex licence under the historical CAR framework and, from November 2025, the relevant EASR authorisation. SEPA assesses the risk, scale, location and sensitivity of the water environment when determining the proper route and conditions.

Scotland project strategy should integrate SEPA authorisation with planning permission, environmental impact assessment where applicable, water abstraction, marine licensing, flood risk, controlled waste, waste carrier requirements, building, fire, habitat, protected sites, land reform, Scottish Water, local authority, health and safety, energy, transport, forestry, fisheries and sectoral approvals. SEPA advises prospective applicants to contact it to discuss an activity before preparing and submitting an authorisation application. A SEPA authorisation does not generally replace the wider project approval portfolio.

Object Definition

Environmental permitting in Scotland, increasingly described as environmental authorisation, is the regulatory approval and compliance function through which a person controlling a regulated activity obtains and maintains the appropriate SEPA authorisation. Under the Environmental Authorisations (Scotland) Regulations 2018, authorisation can take the form of a permit, registration, notification or compliance with general binding rules. The integrated framework applies to industrial, water and waste activities from 1 November 2025 and is supported by conditions, monitoring, reporting, inspection, enforcement, variation, transfer, surrender, suspension and revocation processes.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of Scottish environmental authorisation is to protect and improve the environment by ensuring activities that could cause harm, pollution or resource misuse are properly controlled. EASR applies a proportionate model: low-risk activities may comply with general binding rules, activities with collective or defined risks may need registration, and moderate to higher-risk or complex activities may need a permit with standard or tailored conditions. Authorisations establish legal operating conditions, including environmental protection, monitoring, reporting, management, incident response and change-control requirements.

PurposeTo protect air, land, water, ecosystems, natural resources and human health by authorising and controlling regulated activities through proportionate permits, registrations, notifications, general binding rules, monitoring, reporting, inspection and enforcement.

Jurisdiction Characteristics

Scotland is distinctive because it is implementing a unified environmental-authorisation model through EASR. SEPA states that from 1 November 2025 water, waste-management and industrial activities are regulated under EASR, replacing eight prior regulatory regimes. In most cases, existing authorisations transitioned automatically, with CAR licences and most PPC permits becoming EASR permits. The public register now includes EASR industrial, water and waste authorisation and application documentation issued or received from that transition date.

Operational CultureIntegrated, risk-based and control-led, with SEPA pre-application engagement, activity and operator classification, permits, registrations, notifications, general binding rules, environmental conditions, monitoring, reporting, public registers, inspection and adaptive authorisation management central to project delivery.
Institutional StructureSEPA is Scotland’s principal environmental regulator and administers EASR and legacy or transitional environmental authorisation functions. Scottish planning, marine, local authority, water, energy, forestry, fisheries and sectoral bodies manage connected approvals; UK authorities retain relevant reserved or federal-equivalent functions.
Permit LogicThe activity’s type, scale, location and environmental risk determine whether general binding rules, notification, registration or a permit is required. A registration commonly applies to low individual risk activities that may have collective effects; permits are used for moderate, high-risk or more complex activities and may be simple or complex in water-activity contexts.
Language ExpectationEnglish is the primary language for applications, authorisations, technical evidence, public registers, monitoring and regulator correspondence. Gaelic can be relevant to local community engagement in parts of Scotland; operators should confirm applicable public-participation requirements with SEPA and local authorities.

Applicable Legislation

Scotland’s current integrated framework is based on the Environmental Authorisations (Scotland) Regulations 2018, as expanded to regulate water, waste-management and industrial activities from 1 November 2025. The framework replaces or absorbs major functions previously administered under CAR, PPC, waste-management licensing and other regulations. Existing authorisations and activities can remain subject to transition provisions, legacy documents and activity-specific requirements, so operators should verify the current legal route with SEPA.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

The Scottish route begins with defining the activity, person with control, site and environmental risks. SEPA advises prospective applicants to contact it before preparing an application. The operator determines whether the activity is regulated under EASR, whether general binding rules or notification apply, whether registration or a permit is required and whether legacy or transition arrangements affect the route. The person applying for a permit must have, or will have, control over the regulated activity to be authorised.

1. Define the Person, Activity and SiteIdentify the legal person with, or who will have, control of the regulated activity; the site, activity type, scale, process, emissions, water, discharge, waste, radioactive substances, environmental receptors and intended changes.
2. Contact SEPA Before ApplyingDiscuss the proposed activity with SEPA before preparing and submitting an application to identify authorisation requirements, route, pre-application evidence and relevant environmental concerns.
3. Determine Regulated Activity and Transition StatusAssess whether the activity is an EASR industrial, water, waste or radioactive-substances activity and whether existing CAR, PPC, waste-management or other legacy authorisation has transitioned automatically.
4. Determine Authorisation LevelAssess whether the activity is covered by general binding rules, requires notification, registration or permit, and whether a water activity presents low, collective, moderate, high or complex environmental risk.
5. Identify Connected ApprovalsMap planning permission, environmental impact assessment, marine licensing, water abstraction, Scottish Water, local authority, building, fire, habitat, fisheries, forestry, energy, transport, UK and sectoral approvals.
6. Prepare Technical and Environmental EvidencePrepare application forms, operator-control evidence, site plans, activity descriptions, risk assessments, emissions, water and discharge data, waste or radioactive-substances information, control measures, monitoring, reporting and management material.
7. Submit and Complete SEPA ReviewSubmit the application and supporting information to SEPA using the specified route. SEPA assesses completeness, environmental risk, technical requirements, consultation and public-register requirements and may request further information.
8. Authorisation Decision and ConditionsSEPA grants, registers, records notification, confirms general-binding-rule status, varies, transfers, suspends, revokes, refuses or accepts surrender of the authorisation and sets binding environmental conditions.
9. Operate, Monitor and Manage ChangeOperate within the authorisation, maintain controls, competence and records, monitor and report, respond to inspections and assess variation, transfer, new authorisation or surrender needs before changes, closure or corporate control changes.
Decision logic: Identify the person in control, the activity, location, risk and EASR transition status first. Contact SEPA before applying, then determine whether general binding rules, notification, registration or permit is required and coordinate planning, marine, water, habitat, local and sectoral approvals before construction or operation.

Cross-Border Relevance

Scottish environmental authorisations are territorial and activity- and control-specific, but cross-border relevance is substantial due to England–Scotland operations, North Sea, Atlantic and Irish Sea activity, ports, offshore energy, aquaculture, fisheries, cross-border waste movement, UK-wide supply chains, international investment, water catchments and marine environments. A Scottish SEPA authorisation does not automatically authorise equivalent activities elsewhere in the UK, and authorisations from another UK jurisdiction do not automatically apply in Scotland.

Domestic and International OperatorsScottish, UK, foreign-owned and multinational operators are subject to the same SEPA authorisation, control, competence, monitoring and compliance requirements for regulated activities located in Scotland.
Scotland and UK AllocationScotland has its own SEPA and EASR framework. England, Wales and Northern Ireland use separate environmental regulator and permitting systems, requiring site-specific jurisdiction and regulator analysis.
Marine, Energy and Cross-Border ContextNorth Sea, Atlantic, Irish Sea, ports, offshore wind, oil and gas, marine works, aquaculture, fisheries, cross-border waste movement, water catchments and energy networks can require additional planning, marine, habitat, UK, international and sectoral approvals.
Language ConsiderationsEnglish is the primary regulatory language. Gaelic can be relevant to local community engagement in some parts of Scotland; operators should confirm public consultation and accessible communication requirements with SEPA and local authorities.
Practical RiskAssuming that an authorisation, registration, general-binding-rule status, planning permission or corporate environmental standard issued for another site or UK jurisdiction automatically authorises the activity in Scotland.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central operational risk is incorrect activity, control or authorisation-level classification during Scotland’s integrated-framework transition. A project can require a SEPA EASR permit, registration, notification or general-binding-rule compliance, together with planning, marine, water, Scottish Water, habitat, local authority, UK and sectoral approvals. The person in control, physical activity, site, emissions, water, waste, environmental risks and management arrangements must correspond to the authorisation throughout the facility lifecycle.

Transition RiskWater, waste-management and industrial activities moved into EASR from 1 November 2025. Existing authorisations generally transitioned automatically, but operators should verify exact authorisation type, conditions, holder and activity-specific status.
Control RiskThe person applying for a permit must have, or will have, control over the regulated activity. Changes in ownership, operation, contracts or corporate structure can require transfer, variation or other authorisation action.
Authorisation-Level RiskGeneral binding rules, notifications, registrations and permits serve different risk categories. Incorrect route selection can delay a project or create non-compliance exposure.
Water-Environment RiskSewage, private treatment, abstraction, impoundment, engineering works, aquaculture, pesticide, sheep-dip and other water activities can require separate or detailed water authorisation and site-specific environmental controls.
Evidence RiskIncomplete control, activity, risk, emissions, water, waste, radioactive-substances, site-plan, technical-competence, monitoring or management evidence can delay review, prompt further information requests or lead to restrictive conditions.
Cost DriversPre-application engagement, activity classification, technical studies, risk assessment, water and discharge modelling, waste characterisation, emissions controls, environmental management, application and subsistence fees, monitoring, reporting, consultants, planning and marine coordination, permit variation, transfer, closure and remediation work.

FAQ

What is EASR?EASR refers to the Environmental Authorisations (Scotland) Regulations 2018. The framework authorises regulated activities through permits, registrations, notifications or general binding rules and now provides an integrated route for industrial, water and waste activities.
Who regulates environmental authorisations in Scotland?SEPA is Scotland’s principal environmental regulator and administers environmental authorisations for industrial, water, waste, radioactive-substances and other regulated activities within its competence.
What changed from 1 November 2025?SEPA states that water, waste-management and industrial activities became regulated under EASR from 1 November 2025, replacing eight prior regulatory regimes. In most cases existing authorisations transitioned automatically.
What authorisation levels can apply?Depending on activity type, scale and risk, a regulated activity may need compliance with general binding rules, notification, registration or a permit. Permits are generally used for moderate, high-risk or more complex activities.
Should applicants contact SEPA before applying?Yes. SEPA advises applicants to contact it to discuss the activity before preparing and submitting an application, helping identify the correct authorisation route and supporting evidence.

Environmental permits in Hong Kong operate through a set of ordinance-specific licensing and approval systems administered primarily by the Environmental Protection Department. The applicable route depends on the project, facility, activity and environmental media involved. Large designated projects can require environmental impact assessment and an environmental permit under the Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance, while industrial and trade activities can require licences, permits, written permissions or notifications under air, water, waste, noise and chemical-control legislation.

The Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance applies to designated projects listed in its schedules. A project proponent must obtain an environmental permit before constructing or operating a designated project, unless a statutory exemption, permission to apply directly for permit or other specified route applies. The EIA process is structured around project-profile submission, EIA study brief or direct-permit route, EIA report, public inspection, comments, authority review and permit conditions.

For industrial premises, the Air Pollution Control Ordinance, Water Pollution Control Ordinance, Waste Disposal Ordinance, Noise Control Ordinance and related regulations establish separate licensing, permit and notification requirements. Any person wishing to conduct specified processes must obtain a licence from the Environmental Protection Department. Other common regulatory routes include licences for waste disposal, permits for hazardous waste, discharge licences, construction-dust notifications and technical controls for furnaces, ovens, chimneys and other equipment.

For international businesses, Hong Kong’s compact geography, dense urban setting, port and logistics activity, infrastructure development and close land-use interaction make early site and permit mapping essential. Environmental permit strategy should be coordinated with planning, lease, building, fire, transport, marine, waste, water, occupational-safety and sectoral approvals before construction, installation, commissioning or material operational change.

Object Definition

Environmental permitting in Hong Kong is the regulatory approval and compliance function through which designated projects, industrial processes and potentially polluting activities are assessed, authorised, licensed, permitted, notified and controlled. It includes environmental permits under the Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance, licences for specified processes, air and water pollution controls, waste and chemical permits, noise controls, monitoring, reporting and material-change management.

Purpose and Primary Outcome

The purpose of Hong Kong’s environmental permitting system is to avoid, minimise and control adverse environmental effects from designated projects and to prevent pollution from industrial, commercial, construction and waste activities. The environmental permit and pollution-control licences convert statutory obligations into project- and facility-specific conditions concerning design, construction, operation, monitoring, reporting and environmental management.

PurposeTo protect the environment and public health through environmental impact assessment, project environmental permits, pollution-control licences, discharge conditions, waste management, noise controls, monitoring and enforcement.
Primary OutcomeAn environmental permit, EIA outcome, specified-process licence, water-discharge licence, waste permit, noise permit, notification outcome, amendment, refusal or other decision defining the project or facility’s lawful position.
Business ValueEarly project designation, site, facility and authority mapping supports planning, land and building decisions, engineering, construction, commissioning, operating compliance and management of environmental risk.

Country Characteristics

Hong Kong’s system is distinctive because it combines a highly formal, open EIA permit process for designated projects with detailed media-specific operating licences. Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance documents, study briefs, EIA reports, permit applications and issued permits are maintained in public registers. The Environmental Protection Department also regulates specified industrial processes, water discharges, waste and construction pollution through separate licences, permits and notifications.

Operational CultureCentralised, technically detailed and public-register based, with formal EIA, permit conditions, electronic public access, media-specific operating licences, monitoring, inspection and enforcement.
Institutional StructureThe Environmental Protection Department and Director of Environmental Protection lead EIA permits and pollution control; Planning, Buildings, Marine, Transport and other authorities manage connected project approvals.
Permit LogicDesignated projects require EIA and environmental permit before construction or operation. Industrial and trade premises require the relevant licence, permit or notification under air, water, waste, noise and other environmental ordinances.
Language ExpectationChinese and English are official languages in Hong Kong. Environmental permit, EIA and pollution-control documents may require compliance with official language, public-register and authority requirements.

Applicable Legislation

Hong Kong environmental permitting is organised through the Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance and media-specific pollution-control ordinances. The EIA Ordinance governs designated projects and environmental permits. The Air Pollution Control, Water Pollution Control, Waste Disposal and Noise Control ordinances regulate operating activities, facilities and environmental emissions or discharges. The exact legal route depends on the project and activity.

Process Flow and Decision Tree

The Hong Kong route begins with project and facility classification. The project proponent determines whether the proposal is a designated project under the EIA Ordinance and whether the operating activity requires a specified process licence, water discharge licence, waste permit, noise permit, notification or other pollution-control permission. The Environmental Protection Department then assesses the relevant application and establishes project or operating conditions.

1. Define the Project and ActivityIdentify the site, proponent, operator, designated project status, facility, capacity, construction, process, equipment, emissions, discharges, waste, noise, marine context and intended changes.
2. Screen EIAO StatusDetermine whether the project is a Schedule 2 or Schedule 3 designated project requiring EIA study brief, EIA report and environmental permit or another EIA Ordinance route.
3. Identify Pollution Control RequirementsDetermine whether the operating activity requires specified process licence, air notification, water discharge licence, waste permit, chemical waste registration, noise permit, construction dust notification or other approval.
4. Identify Competent AuthoritiesMap EPD and Director of Environmental Protection functions together with planning, building, marine, water, drainage, transport, fire, waste and sectoral authority roles.
5. Prepare the Evidence BasePrepare project profile, EIA documentation, technical descriptions, site plans, emissions and discharge data, waste information, monitoring, mitigation, environmental management and permit-condition proposals.
6. Submit ApplicationsSubmit environmental permit, licence, permit, notification or technical application through the relevant EPD or government channel and provide supplementary information as requested.
7. Examination and ParticipationThe authority reviews completeness, conducts technical assessment, provides public inspection and comment for EIA documents where required, consults other authorities and evaluates conditions.
8. Decision and ConditionsThe Director or competent authority issues, conditions, refuses, varies, suspends or cancels the environmental permit, licence, permit or other environmental decision.
9. Implement, Monitor and Manage ChangeConstruct and operate within conditions, carry out monitoring and reporting, manage inspections and assess changes to project, facility, emissions, discharges, waste or operating assumptions before implementation.
Decision logic: Determine designated-project status and operating pollution-control requirements first. Then map EPD, planning, building, marine and sectoral approvals. The environmental record must consistently describe the final project, premises, process, emissions, discharges, waste and mitigation measures.

Cross-Border Relevance

Hong Kong environmental permits are territorial and project-specific, but cross-border relevance is substantial because of its integration with the Pearl River Delta, international trade, port and marine operations, cross-boundary infrastructure, regional air quality, water and waste flows, foreign ownership and global supply chains. Hong Kong permits must be assessed independently even where a proponent holds approvals elsewhere.

Foreign CompaniesForeign-owned developers and operators are subject to the same Hong Kong EIA, environmental permit, pollution-control licence and compliance framework as domestic proponents.
Regional ContextPearl River Delta, marine, aviation, port, cross-boundary transport, regional air quality and water contexts can be material to project assessment and environmental conditions.
International Trade ContextHazardous materials, waste, chemicals, shipping, logistics and international supply chains can trigger additional environmental, waste, marine, customs or sectoral requirements.
Language ConsiderationsChinese and English are official languages. Applicants should align technical documentation, public-facing materials and permit applications with the language and format requirements of the competent authority.
Practical RiskAssuming that a permit, EIA, corporate standard or approval from another jurisdiction automatically authorises the project, facility or discharge in Hong Kong.

Operating Constraints, Risks and Costs

The central operational risk is incomplete project and facility classification. A proposal may trigger EIA environmental permit, pollution-control licences and several connected project approvals. Dense land use, neighbours, marine context, technical emissions controls and public-register procedures make accurate design and early authority engagement essential.

Designation RiskA project may be incorrectly assessed as not being a designated project, resulting in failure to undertake required EIA or obtain environmental permit before construction or operation.
Media-Permit RiskA facility may require several separate permits or licences for air, water, specified processes, waste, chemical waste, noise and construction work.
Public-Procedure RiskEIA documents and environmental permit applications can be subject to public inspection, comments, register publication and public-interest scrutiny.
Evidence RiskIncomplete project, emissions, discharge, waste, noise, marine, mitigation, monitoring or site documentation can lead to supplemental requests, delay or restrictive conditions.
Condition RiskEnvironmental permits and licences can impose conditions on construction, operation, emissions, effluent, waste, noise, monitoring, reporting, environmental management and future changes.
Cost DriversEIA studies, technical modelling, marine and water work, emissions and discharge controls, waste management, public consultation, permit management, monitoring systems, mitigation, building coordination, redesign and appeal work.

FAQ

What is an environmental permit in Hong Kong?It is the permit issued by the Director of Environmental Protection under the Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance for construction or operation of a designated project, subject to environmental conditions.
Which projects require EIA and environmental permit?Projects listed as designated projects in the schedules to the Environmental Impact Assessment Ordinance require the applicable EIA process and environmental permit before construction or operation, unless a statutory alternative route applies.
What is a specified process licence?It is a licence required from the Environmental Protection Department for industrial processes listed as specified processes under the Air Pollution Control Ordinance framework.
Can industrial facilities need separate pollution-control licences?Yes. Depending on the facility and activity, separate licences, permits or notifications can apply to air emissions, water discharges, waste, chemical waste, noise, construction dust and other environmental media.
Can an environmental permit be varied?Yes. Changes to project scope, construction, facility, capacity, emissions, discharges, waste, site or environmental measures can require permit variation, further environmental permit or additional licence and notification procedures.
